SimuMarket AI is used at two stages of an F&B business in Greater Jakarta.

## Before the business opens

A prospective owner enters the concept, target location, prices, costs, capital, capacity, and expected volume. The system combines the market evidence that is available, a synthetic persona simulation, deterministic financial calculation, and versioned scoring.

The question it helps answer:

> Is this F&B idea, at that price and location, viable enough to pursue, and which risks must be mitigated?

## After the business is running

The owner sets up the business and its products. Owner or cashier records sales. Once transactions span seven distinct local dates in the `Asia/Jakarta` timezone, the system unlocks basic analytics such as daily sales, product performance, and rule-based insight.

The question it helps answer:

> What is selling, what has not had enough exposure, and which operational aspects need review?

## Three core modules

| Module | Purpose |
|---|---|
| F&B education | Covers legal requirements, pricing, ingredients, and customer acquisition |
| Market Analysis | Produces a readiness report with evidence, finance, risks, and recommendations |
| Transaction Management | Manages products, transactions, receipt review, analytics, and exports |

The product lifecycle is `Learn -> Analyse -> Launch -> Record -> Understand -> Improve`. Confirmed transactions become stronger input for the next analysis, so the product does not stop at being a one-shot calculator.

## The four OASIS councils

- Market Analyst assesses competitor evidence and market conditions.
- Customer Persona runs synthetic persona responses against a scenario.
- Finance critiques financial viability but is not the calculator.
- Report composes the narrative from validated artifacts.

Finance, scoring, and transaction aggregation are always computed by deterministic code, outside any free LLM decision. The division of labour is described in [OASIS and the deterministic engine](/en/docs/arsitektur/oasis-dan-deterministik).

## The Decision Support System boundary

The product assembles material for a decision. It does not make the decision for the user and does not predict real-world business outcomes.

What the system answers:

- how much fixed cost must be covered at a given price and volume assumption;
- break-even and payback across three explicit assumption scenarios;
- which objections different personas raise about the concept, price, and channel;
- which evidence exists, how fresh it is, and which parts are unavailable;
- which risks and data points most need validation before capital is committed.

What the system does not answer:

- guaranteed revenue, real customer counts, or the probability of success;
- investment advice, credit advice, or legal advice;
- a replacement for field surveys, customer interviews, and location observation;
- an absolute "viable" or "not viable" verdict detached from its assumptions.

Every report carries the same disclaimer on screen and in the PDF: the result supports a decision and does not guarantee business success.
